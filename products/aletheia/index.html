aletheia Not released · in design

/əˈleɪθiə/  ·  a-LAY-thee-a

aletheia n.

the state of not being hidden — what shows itself once concealment is removed. From a- + lethe, the river of forgetting: literally, un-forgetting.

also —

knowing what is actually on your Mac: every toolchain, cache and long-forgotten install, with its size, its age, and whether you have touched it since.

A macOS developer tool for cleaning up after ourselves. It finds where developer disk and memory actually go — and then tells you exactly what it would do, so you can do it yourself instead.

What a finding looks like

VS Code has eight extension folders it marked dead and never removed

2.6 GB rebuilt instantly measured seen before · came back in 48 h

VS Code's own .obsolete file names these folders. That is the editor saying they are dead — not this app guessing from a date or a version number.

Do it myself

The exact paths and commands, copyable.

Clean it for me

Runs precisely what is printed alongside.

# eight folders named in ~/.vscode/extensions/.obsolete rm -rf ~/.vscode/extensions/ms-python.python-2025.14.0 rm -rf ~/.vscode/extensions/anthropic.claude-code-1.0.44 # … six more, every path shown in full before anything runs

Both buttons do the same thing, and the app says so. Where a package manager has its own cleanup verb, both routes are literally brew cleanup — there is no version of this you cannot run yourself.

What it will not do

  • NO
    Claim space macOS already manages. Twenty-eight registered CacheDelete services own logs, Photos caches, thumbnails and revisions, and deleted drops them under pressure. Most of what this category advertises is space the OS already owns, and saying so costs us nothing.
  • NO
    Touch anything whose real copy lives elsewhere. Dropbox, iCloud, OneDrive, NAS mounts and every future one are reported and never deleted. A cloud folder is not a cache.
  • NO
    Report a number it cannot stand behind. Every finding is marked measured or inferred and carries the command behind it. Without Full Disk Access it cannot see Trash, Safari, Mail or Messages — and it says that, rather than quietly leaving them out of a total.

The guarantee

It never deletes anything whose authoritative copy is somewhere else. Everything it proposes is something your machine can rebuild on its own.

Why it exists

On one 8 GB MacBook with a 228 GB disk, a careful manual cleanup reclaimed 26 GB. Within 48 hours, 16 GB of it was back. The regrowth is silent, invisible, and faster than anyone remembers to check — which is the part no amount of care fixes on its own.

Every figure on this page was measured on a single machine — one 8 GB M2 running macOS 26.5.2, build 25F84. One machine is not a study, and this page will not pretend otherwise.
